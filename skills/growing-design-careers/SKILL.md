# Growing design careers

Levels differ less in craft than in scope: how big a problem a person can take on, how independently, and how much they raise the work of others.

## 1. Level expectations

A starting point; adapt it to the company's ladder.

| Level | Scope | Independence | Influence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intern, junior** | Tasks and parts of features | With regular guidance | Own work; learns the system and the process |
| **Mid-level** | Features end to end | Independent on known problems | Product trio; brings evidence to decisions |
| **Senior** | Complex features and flows, ambiguous problems | Frames problems, not only solves them | Raises quality in the team; mentors |
| **Lead, staff, principal** | Product areas, cross-team problems, systems | Sets direction | Shapes roadmap and standards across teams |
| **Manager, head, director, CDO** | The team and the organization | Builds the team that does the work | Represents design in company strategy |

Individual contributor and manager tracks should reach the same seniority. Managing is a different job, not a reward for good design.

## 2. Finding the next step

Ask what the person does today and what they want. Compare with the next level's scope and name two or three gaps with concrete actions, for example "run the next kickoff", "own the research plan for the onboarding redesign", "present to the leadership team once this quarter". Pair each with a way to get feedback.

## 3. Feedback

Specific, about the work and its effect, with a suggestion: "In the review, the trade-offs were clear, but the success metric was missing, so the PM could not compare options. Next time open with the metric." Ask for feedback the same way: name what you want feedback on.

## 4. Portfolios and case studies

Show thinking, not only screens. For each case study: the problem and context, your role and the team, the evidence, the options considered, the decisions and why, the result with numbers if available, and what you would do differently. Respect confidentiality: remove client data, ask permission, or describe the work without showing it.

## 5. Interviews

Prepare two or three case studies to walk through in under ten minutes each, with the decisions in the center. For design exercises, frame before sketching (`framing-design-problems`), think aloud, and state assumptions. Ask the team about how design works there: involvement, rituals, ladder, research.

## 6. Writing a career ladder (for managers)

Define levels by scope, independence, influence and craft, with observable behaviors, not years of experience or tool skills. Include research, content and design systems roles if the team has them. Test the ladder on current team members before publishing it, and review it yearly.
