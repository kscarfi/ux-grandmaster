# Running research operations

ResearchOps makes good research repeatable and safe: the right participants, informed consent, protected data, findings people can find again. It serves researchers and everyone else who talks to users.

## 1. Diagnose

Find out who runs research (researchers, designers, PMs), how often, how participants are found, where recordings and notes live, and what happens to findings after the readout. The usual pain points are slow recruiting, scattered findings, and unclear rules for personal data.

## 2. The building blocks

| Block | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Recruiting** | A screener template, a participant panel or vendor, a rule against over-using the same people, and fair incentives. Track who took part and when. |
| **Consent and ethics** | Plain-language consent covering purpose, recording, storage, retention and withdrawal. Extra care with minors, health, finance and vulnerable groups. Follow the laws that apply (for example GDPR in the EU, KVKK in Türkiye); involve legal or privacy staff. |
| **Data handling** | Where recordings and notes are stored, who can access them, how long they are kept, and how personal data is removed from shared findings. Never paste participant data into tools that are not approved for it, including AI tools (`operating-ai-in-design`). |
| **Repository** | One place where findings live as atomic observations and insights with their evidence (`synthesizing-research`), tagged so teams can search before re-asking. |
| **Templates** | Research plan, discussion guide, usability test script, note-taking sheet, synthesis board, readout. |
| **Enablement** | A short training and office hours for non-researchers, with clear limits: what they may run alone (simple usability tests, interviews on known topics) and what needs a researcher (sensitive topics, strategic studies, surveys that drive big decisions). |
| **Tools** | Scheduling, video and transcription, repository, survey tool, with owners and approved data use. |

## 3. Start small

Pick the one block causing the most pain and fix it first, often recruiting or the repository. Write who owns it and how you will know it works (for example, time from request to first session).

## 4. Rules that do not bend

- No research without informed consent.
- No participant data in unapproved tools.
- Simulated or AI-generated participants are not research and never go into the repository as evidence.
