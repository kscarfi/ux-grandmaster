# Taste guardrails

Patterns that make a design look machine-made. Avoid them unless the brief or the design system asks for them.

## Visual

- Purple-to-blue gradients on everything, especially hero backgrounds and buttons.
- Glassmorphism cards floating on a blurred gradient.
- Every element in its own card with a shadow, cards inside cards.
- Emoji used as icons in a product interface.
- A row of three identical feature cards with an icon, a title and two lines of text, as the default answer to any layout.
- Huge rounded corners on everything, regardless of the system's radius scale.
- Centered body text in long paragraphs.
- Low-contrast grey text "for elegance".
- Decorative blobs, sparkles and abstract 3D shapes with no meaning.
- A dark mode that is pure black with pure white text.
- Fake data that is too perfect: round numbers, every name "John Doe", every chart going up.
- Fake device chrome: drawn status bars, notches and home indicators on a design that will never ship with them.

## Copy

- Hype words: revolutionary, seamless, magic, effortless, supercharge, unlock, elevate, delightful.
- Em dashes used as all-purpose punctuation.
- "Welcome to the future of …" headlines.
- Exclamation marks in system messages.
- Button labels that do not say what happens: "Submit", "OK", "Continue" when a specific verb fits.
- Error messages that blame the user or show a code instead of a fix.

## Structure

- Adding a feature, a setting or an onboarding carousel when removing something would solve the problem.
- A dashboard of every available metric, with no hierarchy and no question it answers.
- Modals for things that could be inline.
- One design for the happy path only: no empty, loading, error or long-content states.

When the team's brand or design system deliberately uses one of these, follow the system and note the exception.
