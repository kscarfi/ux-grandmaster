# UX authorities

When a finding or decision needs support, cite the strongest source that applies. Read the source; do not cite from memory. Paraphrase, quote briefly, and give the title, URL and date read.

## Order of strength

1. **Evidence from this product's own users:** research, analytics, support tickets, tests. Nothing outranks what real users of this product did.
2. **Standards and legal requirements:** WCAG 2.2 (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/), and laws such as the European Accessibility Act or the ADA where they apply.
3. **Platform owners' guidelines:** Apple HIG, Material Design and Android design guidance, Meta Horizon OS design, Windows and Fluent. See `following-platform-guidelines`.
4. **Research organizations with published methods:**
   - Nielsen Norman Group, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ (usability heuristics, research methods, eyetracking, UX for many domains).
   - Baymard Institute, https://baymard.com/research (e-commerce and checkout usability; much of it is paid, cite only what is publicly readable).
   - GOV.UK Design System and Service Manual, https://design-system.service.gov.uk (patterns tested with the public, strong on forms and accessibility).
5. **Peer-reviewed research:** ACM CHI and other HCI venues, for questions where it exists.
6. **Books and practitioner guides:** for principles, named with author and title (for example Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics, Jeff Gothelf's *Lean UX*, Adham Dannaway's *Practical UI*, Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger's *Refactoring UI*, Susan Weinschenk's *100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People*). If the user's design library is set up, search it with `uxg library search` and cite the page.
7. **Articles and blog posts:** useful for ideas, weak as evidence. Check the author and date.

## Rules

- Prefer the most specific source: a platform's page on buttons beats a general article on buttons.
- Check dates. A 2015 mobile study may not hold today.
- Do not stretch a source: an article about e-commerce checkout is weak evidence for a medical app.
- When sources disagree, say so and prefer the higher one in this list, or evidence from the product's users.
